Colour options for the menu and the whole UI

Santhosh asked for a neat, professional, very modern look; green is not mandatory, but if we keep green it should be lighter. Below are four palettes, each drawn as the same mini website: side menu, a Diary meal table with the three Remaining states, and two Add food rows. Each shows its full token list, calculated contrast, and pros and cons. The mock-ups follow your device's light or dark setting.

Recommendation: Option B, Calm Teal. It is calm and professional, keeps the table header unmistakable, has one accent colour, and leaves clear room between brand, status and macro colours. If Santhosh wants to keep green, choose Option A (Fresh Mint). Santhosh decides; nothing in the main screens file has changed yet.

Rules that hold in every option

  • Remaining always pairs colour with words: "742 left", "18 over", "20 over". Colour is never the only cue.
  • Three steps: under goal, slightly over (up to 10% above), well over (more than 10% above).
  • Macro colours (carbs, fat, protein, fiber) are at least 20 apart from each other (colour distance) and clearly different from the brand; the orange fat and the red "well over" are the closest pair to a status colour (about 10 apart), so the words always carry the meaning. Where a macro colour changed, the option says so.
  • Text needs 4.5:1; borders, focus rings and bars need 3:1 (WCAG 2.2 AA). All figures below were calculated, not estimated.

New tokens needed (any option)

  • --brand-ink: links, secondary buttons, focus ring (differs from --brand in A and C-dark).
  • --field-border: input and outline-button border at 3:1 (the old --border is only 1.5:1).
  • --nav-bar, --nav-cta-bg, --nav-cta-text, --nav-border: replace the green gradient and the on-dark button.
  • --th-line, --logo-a, --logo-b, --logo-ink.
  • Status colours are re-tuned (darker) in all options.

Pros

  • Closest to what Santhosh already knows: green stays, so the name and the product feel connected.

Cons

  • The light menu is separated from the page only by a thin line and a tint, so the structure is softer.

Macro colours

Carbs (blue #2563eb) and fat (orange #c2410c) are unchanged. D. Graphite & Lime (neutral graphite + one lime spark)

Mood: Sharp, sporty and editorial: graphite and white, with a single lime highlight.

Why this fourth option: I first considered warm coral or saffron nodding to Indian food, but tested it and rejected it: an orange or coral brand collides with the orange fat macro and the red "well over" state, and a saffron brand collides with the amber "slightly over" state. Remaining status would then be ambiguous. A neutral graphite brand removes every collision, lets the nutrition colours speak, and looks the most current and premium.

Pros

  • Neutral brand means the four macro colours and the three status colours do all the colour work; nothing can clash.
  • Very modern and editorial; the single lime highlight is distinctive and memorable.
  • Highest text contrast of the four (primary button 17.7:1).

Cons

  • A near-black primary button can feel stern for a health app.
  • Lime can only be used on dark surfaces (never as text on white), so it appears only in the menu and header line.
  • Dark menu is heavy, as today. In dark mode the primary button flips to white-on-black.
  • Least warmth or "food" feeling.

Macro colours

Carbs (blue #2563eb) and fat (orange #c2410c) are unchanged. Protein changes from violet #7c3aed to deep indigo-violet #3b1a85 (dark mode #8160f6) and fiber changes from teal #0f766e to magenta #c026d3 (dark #f472d0). The brand is neutral graphite, so no macro can clash with it.

Separation measured as perceptual colour distance (OKLab, x100; 20 or more is clearly different at dot size; every macro pair meets it). Light mode: closest macro pair 22.7 (carbs / protein); closest macro to a status colour 10.1 (fat / error); closest macro to the brand 20.5 (protein / brand); the three status colours are 14.6 or more apart; brand to success green 29.7. Dark mode: 22.0 / 10.6 / 20.4 / 18.7. Macros always carry a written label, and macro colours are used only for dots, bars and header underlines (3:1 is required for those), never as text colour, so colour is never the only cue.

Remaining status colours (words always shown)

  • Under goal --success #14703a: shows "742 left".
  • Slightly over (up to 10%) --warning #8a5a00: shows "18 over".
  • Well over (more than 10%) --error #a8102a: shows "20 over".

Chosen for this palette: darker than Round 4 so they pass 4.5:1 even on tinted badges and the Remaining row, and the red is a deeper crimson so it never reads as the orange fat macro.
